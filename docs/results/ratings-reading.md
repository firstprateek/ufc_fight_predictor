Written by hand after the run; the numbers above are generated. This text is `docs/results/ratings-reading.md`, passed to `ufc ratings-report --reading`.

**Summary.** The new skill ratings and context features make the models better, and the improvement holds out of sample (on development, where the choices were made, `hgb` alone improved by -0.0046 [-0.0097, +0.0004], not distinguishable from zero), but they do not close the gap to the betting market. On the holdout, `all` beats `base` for every model with an interval that excludes zero (`hgb` -0.0088, logistic regression -0.0116, `blend` -0.0100 log loss). The market is still about 0.04 log loss better than the best model (`blend`, +0.0430 on the holdout, +0.0381 on the full period). Whether the model carries information the market lacks is a split result: no evidence on development, a small positive result on the holdout, none on the full period.

- **What was kept.** The pre-registered rule dropped nothing: no group was worse than `base` for `hgb` with an interval above zero (all three point estimates were negative, -0.0014 to -0.0022, with intervals that include zero). So the final configuration is `all`. Individually, no group had an interval that excludes zero for `hgb`; for logistic regression only `ratings` did, and for `blend` `ratings` and `context` did. `adjusted` helped by about 0.001 and was never distinguishable from zero. That is the honest answer on the main idea of this task: opponent-adjusted offence and defence ratings added little in these data. The rule keeps a group that does no harm; it is not evidence that the group helps.
- **Who gains.** Logistic regression gains most (the two new rating diffs are strong linear inputs); `hgb` gains less, and its development interval for `all` against `base` includes zero (-0.0046 [-0.0097, +0.0004]). The development gain of `blend` (-0.0057) is also smaller than the holdout gain (-0.0100). Part of the holdout gain may be that 2023 on has more fights per fighter to rate; we did not test that.
- **Against the market.** Model minus market log loss, `all`: `hgb` +0.0395 (development), +0.0484 (holdout), +0.0419 (full); `blend` +0.0363, +0.0430, +0.0381. All intervals are far above zero. `base` was +0.0480 (`hgb`) and +0.0459 (`blend`) on the full period, so the new features recovered roughly an eighth (`hgb`) to a sixth (`blend`) of the gap.
- **Information test.** On development the stack of market and model improves on the market alone by 0.0004 to 0.0005 (`all`), intervals include zero. On the holdout the improvement is 0.0040 (`hgb`) and 0.0048 (`blend`) with intervals that exclude zero ([-0.0068, -0.0013] and [-0.0076, -0.0021]); for `base` the holdout improvement was 0.0024 (`hgb`, includes zero) and 0.0031 (`blend`, excludes zero). On the full period (2016 on) it is 0.0015 and 0.0017 with intervals that include zero, essentially the same as before these changes (0.0017 and 0.0016). Reading: there is weak evidence, in one period, that the model adds something small (a few thousandths of log loss) to the market; it did not show up in development, it is not specific to the new features (`base` shows a similar holdout effect), and the full-period test does not support it. Four holdout comparisons were made and none is corrected for that, and the file holds well over 20 intervals in all (ablation, decision rule, holdout, market): the individual-group intervals that exclude zero (for example `blend` with `context`, upper end -0.0001) should not be treated as findings. We would not rely on it.
- **Caveats.** The odds are pre-fight moneylines of unknown book and timing and cover 88.6% of scored fights (blank odds in the source, mostly 2023-2024 according to `docs/results/market.md`, drop out of every market row; the holdout market rows use 1,398 of 2,030 fights). The holdout therefore leans on a subset whose selection we did not study. Intervals resample events and assume fights are independent given the event.
- **Protocol.** The configuration was frozen before the holdout was run for it, and nothing was changed afterwards. The holdout of the frozen configuration was then seen twice (this report and a default `ufc backtest` run of the same code, which matched); no choice depended on either.
